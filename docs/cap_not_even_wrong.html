"consistent, available, partition-tolerant: pick two" — not even wrong

Car

You’re choosing a car: ferrari or jeep?[1]

There’s plenty to consider: speed, efficiency, how good it’ll look on your driveway, and so on. But you’re preoccupied by two things: Would you make your commute in under an hour? Would you spill your coffee on the way?

You’ve drawn up a table:

potholes no potholes

ferrari

<1hr, spill

<1hr, no spill

jeep

>1hr, no spill

<1hr, no spill

Either car gives sub-hour, spill-free commutes when no potholes. But when there are potholes your commutes can be sub-hour or spill-free but not both. Tradeoff!

You choose a car.

Of any commutes, it makes sense to ask

  1. Were they sub-hour?

  2. Were they spill-free?

but not

  1. Were they pothole-tolerant?

Of your car, it makes sense to ask

  1. Is it pothole-tolerant, time-wise? (ferrari yes, jeep no)

  2. Is it pothole-tolerant, spill-wise? (ferrari no, jeep yes)

but not

  1. Is it pothole-tolerant?

  2. Is it sub-hour?

  3. Is it spill-free?

"Sub-hour, spill-free, pothole-tolerant: pick two"? It doesn’t make sense.

CAP

You’re choosing an algorithm: algo1 or algo2?

There’s plenty to consider: speed, efficiency, how good it’ll look on your CV, and so on. But you’re preoccupied by two things: Would your service be available? Would it be consistent?

You’ve drawn up a table:

partition no partition

algo1

available, inconsistent

available, consistent

algo2

unavailable, consistent

available, consistent

Either algorithm gives an available, consistent service when no partition. But when there is a partition your service can be available or consistent but not both. Tradeoff!

You choose an algorithm.

Of any executions, it makes sense to ask

  1. Were they available?

  2. Were they consistent?

but not

  1. Were they partition-tolerant?

Of your algorithm, it makes sense to ask

  1. Is it partition-tolerant, availability-wise? (algo1 yes, algo2 no)

  2. Is it partition-tolerant, consistency-wise? (algo1 no, algo2 yes)

but not

  1. Is it partition-tolerant?

  2. Is it available?

  3. Is it consistent?

"Available, consistent, partition-tolerant: pick two"? It doesn’t make sense.

1. This note develops one of Martin Kleppmann’s many criticisms of CAP in his great post https://martin.kleppmann.com/2015/05/11/please-stop-calling-databases-cp-or-ap.html. What’s not even wrong is the slogan. The theorem behind the slogan is right enough, but that’s the best that can be said for it. See Kleppmann’s post.
