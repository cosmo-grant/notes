= _"consistent, available, partition-tolerant: pick two"_ &mdash; not even wrong
:published: 2026-11-13

== Car

You're choosing a car: ferrari or jeep?footnote:[
This note develops one of Martin Kleppmann's many criticisms of CAP in his great post https://martin.kleppmann.com/2015/05/11/please-stop-calling-databases-cp-or-ap.html.
What's not even wrong is the _slogan_.
The _theorem_ behind the slogan is right enough, but that's the best that can be said for it.
See Kleppmann's post.]

There's plenty to consider: speed, efficiency, how good it'll look on your driveway, and so on.
But you're preoccupied by two things:
Would you make your commute in under an hour?
Would you spill your coffee on the way?

You've drawn up a table:

[cols="h,1,1", options="header"]
|===
|        | potholes       | no potholes
| ferrari | <1hr, spill    | <1hr, no spill
| jeep    | >1hr, no spill | <1hr, no spill
|===

Either car gives sub-hour, spill-free commutes when no potholes.
But when there are potholes your commutes can be sub-hour or spill-free but not both.
Tradeoff!

You choose a car.

Of any _commutes_, it makes sense to ask

[start=1]
. Were they sub-hour?
. Were they spill-free?

but _not_

[start=3]
. Were they pothole-tolerant?

Of your _car_, it makes sense to ask

[start=4]
. Is it pothole-tolerant, time-wise? (ferrari yes, jeep no)
. Is it pothole-tolerant, spill-wise? (ferrari no, jeep yes)

but _not_

[start=6]
. Is it pothole-tolerant?
. Is it sub-hour?
. Is it spill-free?

_"Sub-hour, spill-free, pothole-tolerant: pick two"?_
It doesn't make sense.

== CAP

You're choosing an algorithm: algo1 or algo2?

There's plenty to consider: speed, efficiency, how good it'll look on your CV, and so on.
But you're preoccupied by two things:
Would your service be available?
Would it be consistent?

You've drawn up a table:

[cols="h,1,1", options="header"]
|===
|       | partition               | no partition
| algo1 | available, inconsistent | available, consistent
| algo2 | unavailable, consistent | available, consistent
|===

Either algorithm gives an available, consistent service when no partition.
But when there is a partition your service can be available or consistent but not both.
Tradeoff!

You choose an algorithm.

Of any _executions_, it makes sense to ask

[start=1]
. Were they available?
. Were they consistent?

but _not_

[start=3]
. Were they partition-tolerant?

Of your _algorithm_, it makes sense to ask

[start=4]
. Is it partition-tolerant, availability-wise? (algo1 yes, algo2 no)
. Is it partition-tolerant, consistency-wise? (algo1 no, algo2 yes)

but _not_

[start=6]
. Is it partition-tolerant?
. Is it available?
. Is it consistent?

_"Available, consistent, partition-tolerant: pick two"?_
It doesn't make sense.
